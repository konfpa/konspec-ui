Closing an order short

SOP-PUR-009 · Revision 2 · Effective 01 Apr 2026

An order is closed short when the balance quantity will never be received — the vendor cannot supply it, the requirement has gone, or the material has been bought elsewhere.

Before you close it

Closing short releases the committed value back to the budget and stops the order appearing in the pending-receipt register. It does not cancel anything already received and it does not touch an invoice that has been booked.

A short close is reversible for seven days.

After that the order is archived and reopening it is a request to Materials.

Tell the vendor before you close, not after. The order is what they are producing against, and a closure they hear about from the accounts department two weeks later is a claim.

Do not close an order short to make the pending register look clean at month end.

The commitment is what the plant is planning cash against. A closed order that is still coming is a payment nobody has provided for.

Where the balance is genuinely still expected but late, the answer is a revised delivery date on the order, which keeps the commitment and moves it out of the overdue count.

Who may close

The buyer who raised the order, up to a released value of ₹1,00,000. Above that it is the plant head, on the same screen, and the released figure is shown before the confirmation.

At Vapi a short close above ₹50,000 also needs the Finance controller.

Silvassa has no second approval on a short close. Everything else on this page is the same at both plants.

Both routes record who closed it and why, and the reason is printed on the order's own document. It is read by the vendor, so write it as something a vendor can read.
